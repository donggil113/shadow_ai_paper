\section{Setup: decision-censored data}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data-generating structure.} Each unit has covariates $X\in\X$, latent
decision-maker information $S\in\mathcal{S}$, a binary decision
$T\in\{0,1\}$ and a binary outcome $Y\in\{0,1\}$. The decision is taken by a
policy that sees both $X$ and $S$,
\[
  T \mid X, S \;\sim\; \text{Bernoulli}\big(\pi(X,S)\big),
\]
and the outcome is recorded \emph{only} when $T=1$. The analyst observes
$n$ i.i.d.\ copies of
\[
  O \;=\; (X,\; T,\; T\cdot Y),
\]
never $S$, and never $Y$ on $\{T=0\}$. We write $P$ for the joint law of
$(X,S,T,Y)$ and $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$ for the induced law of $O$.

\paragraph{What is and is not identified.} Two functionals of $P$ are identified
from $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$:
\[
  e(x) \;=\; \Prb(T=1\mid X=x),
  \qquad
  p_1(x) \;=\; \Prb(Y=1 \mid X=x,\,T=1).
\]
The deployment target is not:
\[
  p(x) \;=\; \Prb(Y=1\mid X=x) \;=\; e(x)\,p_1(x) + \big(1-e(x)\big)\,p_0(x),
  \qquad p_0(x) = \Prb(Y=1\mid X=x, T=0),
\]
because $p_0$ never appears in $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$. For a score
$f:\X\to\R$ and a loss $\ell(z,y)$ the object we care about is the
\emph{deployment risk} $R_P(f) = \E_P[\ell(f(X),Y)]$, taken over all units
including the censored ones --- not the \emph{observed risk}
$\E_P[\ell(f(X),Y)\mid T=1]$ that every practitioner actually measures.

\subsection{The decision-censoring sensitivity model}

\begin{definition}[$\dcsm(\Gamma)$]
\label{def:dcsm}
For $\Gamma\ge 1$, the law $P$ satisfies $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ if
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma^{-1}
  \;\le\;
  \frac{\odds\big(p_0(x)\big)}{\odds\big(p_1(x)\big)}
  \;\le\;
  \Gamma
  \qquad\text{for $P_X$-almost every } x,
  \label{eq:dcsm}
\end{equation}
equivalently $\big|\logit p_0(x) - \logit p_1(x)\big| \le \log\Gamma$, where
$\odds(u) = u/(1-u)$.
\end{definition}

The parameter has a direct reading: \emph{after conditioning on everything the
model sees, the decision maker's private information may shift the outcome
log-odds of the units they censored by at most $\log\Gamma$.} $\Gamma=1$ is
selection on observables (missing at random); $\Gamma=\infty$ imposes nothing and
returns the Manski bounds. The model is a decision-censoring analogue of
Rosenbaum's sensitivity analysis \citep{rosenbaum1983assessing,
rosenbaum2002observational} and is equivalent to Tan's marginal sensitivity
model \citep{tan2006distributional} on the decision propensity:

\begin{lemma}[Equivalence with the marginal sensitivity model]
\label{lem:msm}
Suppose the propensity obeys $\textup{MSM}(\Lambda)$, i.e.
$\Lambda^{-1}\le \odds(e(x))/\odds(\pi(x,s)) \le \Lambda$ for all $x,s$. Then
$P$ satisfies $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ with $\Gamma=\Lambda^{2}$, and the bound is
attained: every value in $[\Lambda^{-2},\Lambda^{2}]$ is realised by some
admissible $\pi$.
\end{lemma}

\Cref{lem:msm} (proved in \cref{app:setup}) lets us work directly on the outcome
side, which is where the functionals we care about live. Everything below is
stated in terms of $\Gamma$; translate with $\Gamma=\Lambda^2$ if the
sensitivity analysis is elicited on the propensity scale.

\subsection{The identified set is a box}

Parameterise the deviation by a \emph{tilt} $a:\X\to[\Gamma^{-1},\Gamma]$ with
$\odds(p_0(x)) = a(x)\,\odds(p_1(x))$. The induced outcome regression is
\begin{equation}
  p^{a}(x)
  \;=\;
  e(x)\,p_1(x)
  \;+\;
  \big(1-e(x)\big)\,\sigma\!\big(\logit p_1(x) + \log a(x)\big),
  \label{eq:tilt}
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ the logistic function. Since \eqref{eq:tilt} is strictly increasing in
$a(x)$ and the constraint acts \emph{pointwise} --- $a$ is an otherwise
unrestricted measurable function --- we get:

\begin{lemma}[Box form of the identified set]
\label{lem:box}
Under $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ the identified set for the deployment regression is
\[
  \I_\Gamma(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
  \;=\;
  \Big\{\, p:\X\to[0,1] \;\Big|\; \plo(x) \le p(x) \le \pup(x)
  \ \text{ for a.e. } x \,\Big\},
\]
with
\[
  \plo(x) = e p_1 + (1-e)\,\sigma\big(\logit p_1 - \log\Gamma\big),
  \qquad
  \pup(x) = e p_1 + (1-e)\,\sigma\big(\logit p_1 + \log\Gamma\big),
\]
all evaluated at $x$. The set is non-empty, convex and compact; every $p$ in it
is realised by an admissible law; and $\Gamma\mapsto\I_\Gamma$ is nested
increasing with $\I_1 = \{p_1\}$ and
$\I_\infty = \{\,e p_1 \le p \le e p_1 + 1 - e\,\}$ (Manski).
\end{lemma}

Two consequences drive everything that follows. First, because the set is a
\emph{product} of intervals, optimisation over it decouples across $x$ for any
separable functional --- this is what makes \cref{sec:learning} tractable.
Second, because $\Gamma$ enters \eqref{eq:tilt} as a pure \emph{translation on
the log-odds scale}, estimation error in $p_1$ propagates to the bounds without
amplification --- this is what makes the $\log\Gamma$ of
\cref{sec:generalization} $\log\Gamma$ rather than $\Gamma$.

We write $\tilde p = (\plo+\pup)/2$ for the midpoint and
$\delta = \pup - \plo$ for the \emph{identification width}, which will act
throughout as a per-unit signal of how much the censoring decision can hide.

\begin{remark}[Directional $\dcsm$]
\label{rem:directional}
In most applications the analyst knows the \emph{sign} of the selection effect
even when its magnitude is unknown: clinicians order tests on the patients they
already suspect are sick, so untested patients are lower risk
($p_0 \le p_1$); lenders fund the applicants they judge safest, so rejected
applicants are higher risk ($p_0 \ge p_1$). Imposing the known sign is a weaker
commitment than naming a number for $\Gamma$, and it replaces the constraint set
by $a\in[\Gamma^{-1},1]$ or $a\in[1,\Gamma]$, halving the width. Every result
below holds verbatim with the correspondingly one-sided box; we report both.
\end{remark}
