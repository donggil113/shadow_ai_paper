\paragraph{Falsifying $\Gamma$.} Across $\nExpFourNCells$ SL-Bench
configurations varying the true $\Gamma_0$, the spread of leniency and whether
decision makers rely on their private information to the same degree
(\cref{tab:falsify}, \cref{app:falsify}), two properties hold without
exception. \emph{Validity}: $\Gamma_{\min}\le\Gamma_0^{\mathrm{cond}}$ in every
cell, as \cref{prop:falsify} requires. \emph{No false alarms}: when the
mechanism is missing-at-random the test returns exactly $1$ and refutes
nothing. On the log scale it recovers $\nExpFourRecoverMin$--$\nExpFourRecoverMax$
of the truth, less when decision makers differ in \emph{how much} they use
their private information rather than only in leniency, which is exactly what
makes their identified sets overlap at a smaller $\Gamma$. At $\Gamma_0=3$ it
already refutes $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ for every $\Gamma<\nExpFourGminAtThree$, which
rules out missing-at-random --- and with it IPW and AIPW --- decisively.

\paragraph{Break-even analysis.} The deliverable a practitioner wants is the
$\Gamma$ at which a conclusion flips. On Lending Club the incumbent model's
AUROC lower bound stays above $0.55$ up to $\Gamma=\nExpFourLendingBreakEvenFiftyFive$
and above chance up to $\Gamma=\nExpFourLendingBreakEvenFifty$ --- so the
claim ``this model ranks better than chance'' survives past the true
$\Gamma_0=\nExpTwoLendingGammaZero$, while ``AUROC is at least $0.60$'' does
not survive even $\Gamma=\nExpFourLendingBreakEvenSixty$. The DCL model's
certified worst-case risk beats the incumbent's at every $\Gamma$ tested, up to
$\Gamma=\nExpFourLendingDclBeatsUpto$: the ordering of models under the
certificate is robust even where the absolute numbers are not.

\paragraph{When $\Gamma$ is set too small} the failure is graceful
(\cref{app:misspec}): over a $\nExpFourMisRange$-fold range of $\Gamma$,
coverage is $\nExpFourMisCovHigh$ for every $\Gamma$ at least half the truth
and falls to $\nExpFourMisCovLow$ below that, while the width grows
monotonically and slowly from $\nExpFourMisWidthMin$ to $\nExpFourMisWidthMax$.
The price of a conservative $\Gamma$ is a wider interval, not a wrong one; the
price of an aggressive one is a graded loss of coverage, not a cliff.
