\section{Decision-censored learning}
\label{sec:learning}

We now stop auditing and start training. Rather than minimising the observed
risk --- the risk on whichever sub-population the incumbent policy chose to
label --- we minimise the worst case over everything the data cannot rule out:
\begin{equation}
  \hat f
  \;\in\;
  \argmin_{f\in\F}\;
  \sup_{P\in\I_\Gamma(P_{\mathrm{obs}})} R_P(f),
  \qquad
  R_P(f) = \E_P\big[\ell(f(X),Y)\big].
  \label{eq:dcl}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The inner supremum, in closed form}

Because $R_P(f)$ is \emph{affine} in $p(x)$ and $\I_\Gamma$ is a product of
intervals (\cref{lem:box}), the supremum decouples across $x$ and is attained at
a vertex. Writing $\ell_1 = \ell(f(x),1)$, $\ell_0 = \ell(f(x),0)$ and using
$\max(a,b)=\tfrac{a+b}{2}+\tfrac{|a-b|}{2}$:

\begin{theorem}[Decision-censored ERM]
\label{thm:dcl}
Let $\ell(\cdot,y)$ be convex for each $y$ and define the \emph{label gap}
$g_\ell(z) = |\ell(z,1)-\ell(z,0)|$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textup{(Exact reduction.)}
\begin{equation}
  \Rbar_\Gamma(f)
  \;:=\;
  \sup_{P\in\I_\Gamma} R_P(f)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\E\Big[\tilde p(X)\,\ell_1 + \big(1-\tilde p(X)\big)\ell_0\Big]}
             _{\text{risk at the midpoint model}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\tfrac12\,\E\Big[\delta(X)\,g_\ell\big(f(X)\big)\Big]}
             _{\text{margin penalty $\times$ identification width}} .
  \label{eq:wc-decomp}
\end{equation}
For the logistic loss $\ell(z,1)-\ell(z,0) = -z$ exactly, so the penalty is
$\tfrac12\E[\delta(X)\,|f(X)|]$: an $L_1$ penalty on the \emph{score}, weighted
by how censored each region of covariate space is.

\item[(b)] \textup{(Exact convexity --- no relaxation needed.)} $\Rbar_\Gamma$
is a pointwise supremum of functions convex in $z$, hence convex in $z$; over
any convex class $\F$ of real-valued scores, \eqref{eq:dcl} is an exactly convex
program. Separately, the penalty term of \eqref{eq:wc-decomp} is convex on its
own \emph{iff} $g_\ell$ is convex --- true for the logistic, squared and
exponential losses, false for the hinge. The distinction matters only for
modular implementation, not for tractability.

\item[(c)] \textup{(Saddle point.)} $R_P(f)$ is convex in $f$ and linear, hence
concave, in $p$, over a convex compact set, so by Sion's theorem
\citep{sion1958general} $\inf_f\sup_P = \sup_P\inf_f$ and a saddle point
$(\hat f, P^\star)$ exists. The adversary's best response is the explicit vertex
\[
  p^\star(x) \;=\; \pup(x)\ \text{ if } \ell(f(x),1)\ge\ell(f(x),0),
  \qquad \plo(x) \text{ otherwise,}
\]
--- for the logistic loss, $\pup$ exactly where the model predicts negative.
$\hat f$ is thus the Bayes predictor for the least-favourable member of the
identified set.

\item[(d)] \textup{(Bayes act.)} For the logistic loss the pointwise minimiser
of \eqref{eq:wc-decomp} is
\begin{equation}
  f^\star_\Gamma(x)
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \logit \plo(x), & \plo(x) > \tfrac12,\\[2pt]
    \logit \pup(x), & \pup(x) < \tfrac12,\\[2pt]
    0, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:bayes-act}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\Cref{eq:bayes-act} is the \emph{interval-shrunk logit} (\cref{fig:rules}): log-odds pulled toward
the decision boundary by exactly the identification width, and pinned at $0$ on
the \emph{abstention band} $\{x : \plo(x)\le\tfrac12\le\pup(x)\}$ where the data
cannot resolve the sign of the optimal decision. At $\Gamma=1$ it degenerates to
$\logit p(x)$, as it must.

\begin{remark}[Contrast with distributional robustness]
\label{rem:dro}
In $f$-divergence and Wasserstein DRO \citep{namkoong2016stochastic,
duchi2021learning, sagawa2020distributionally} the ambiguity set is a ball
around the empirical distribution, chosen for analytical convenience, and the
inner supremum is tractable only after a duality step. Here the ambiguity set is
not chosen --- it is \emph{exactly what the data fail to determine} --- and the
inner supremum has a closed form. \Cref{thm:dcl}(b) is therefore a stronger
statement than "admits a convex relaxation": there is nothing to relax.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Minimax regret, also in closed form}

Minimax risk is conservative by design: it is dominated by how hard the problem
is, not by how much worse the model is than it needed to be. The decision-theoretic
alternative is \emph{regret} --- excess risk relative to the best predictor for
whichever member of $\I_\Gamma$ turns out to be true. For the logistic loss
$R_p(f) - \inf_g R_p(g) = \E[\KL(p\,\|\,\sigma(f))]$, so the objective becomes
$\min_q \max_{p\in[\plo,\pup]}\KL(p\|q)$ pointwise.

\begin{proposition}[Minimax-regret rule]
\label{prop:regret}
Let $H(u) = -u\log u - (1-u)\log(1-u)$. The pointwise minimax-regret score is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \logit q^\star(x)
  \;=\;
  \frac{H\big(\plo(x)\big) - H\big(\pup(x)\big)}{\pup(x)-\plo(x)}\;}
  \label{eq:regret-rule}
\end{equation}
with the convention $q^\star = p$ when $\plo=\pup=p$. The value of the game is
$\KL(\plo\|q^\star) = \KL(\pup\|q^\star)$.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{eq:regret-rule} is a difference quotient of the binary entropy --- a
discrete version of $-H'(u) = \logit u$, to which it degenerates as the box
collapses. Unlike \eqref{eq:bayes-act} it has \emph{no abstention band}: it
remains a genuine probability estimate, which is what a downstream decision rule
usually wants. In our experiments it gives up almost nothing in realised
deployment accuracy while cutting certified regret several-fold, and on real
COMPAS decisions it beats every baseline except the parametric selection model
on realised risk --- the oracle included --- while certifying a regret an order of
magnitude tighter than any of them.

\subsection{Two safety properties}

\begin{corollary}[Validity of the certificate]
\label{cor:validity}
If the true law satisfies $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ then
$R_{P}(f) \le \Rbar_\Gamma(f)$ for every $f$. The certified number is therefore
an upper bound on the risk actually incurred at deployment --- something no
observed-data metric provides.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Budgeted $\dcsm$]
\label{rem:budget}
Pointwise $\Gamma$ permits \emph{every} unit to be maximally tilted at once,
which can be pessimistic. Adding an average-tilt budget
$\E|\log a(X)|\le B$ breaks the decoupling; the supremum is then recovered by
its one-dimensional dual,
$\min_{\lambda\ge0}\{\lambda B + \E[\sup_t\{r(X,t)-\lambda|t|\}]\}$, with
$\lambda$ found by bisection. Strong duality holds ($a\equiv 1$ is a Slater
point). The resulting risk interpolates smoothly between $\Gamma=1$ and
pointwise $\Gamma$; see \cref{app:budget}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ranking-robust learning: harder, and apparently unnecessary}

\Cref{thm:dcl} makes robust \emph{risk} minimisation convex. Robust
\emph{ranking} is not: maximising $\min_{p\in\I_\Gamma}\AUC(f,p)$ is a max-min
over the fractional objective of \cref{sec:ranking}, whose inner problem is
neither concave nor separable in $p$. What makes it approachable at all is that
\cref{thm:sharp-auc} supplies an \emph{exact} $O(n\log n)$ oracle for that inner
problem, so one can run a Danskin best-response scheme: alternate the exact
adversarial $p$ with a gradient step on a smoothed pairwise surrogate weighted by
$p(x)(1-p(x'))$. We implement this as \textsc{DCLRanker}
(\cref{alg:ranker}), with fictitious-play averaging of the adversary --
best-responding to the \emph{latest} adversary oscillates, because the exact
inner argmin inverts the soft labels relative to the current ranking -- and with
best-iterate selection under the exact oracle, so the returned model is never
worse than its initialisation.

We report a negative result. Across our instances the scheme never improved on
its own warm start, the \emph{plug-in midpoint ranking} $x\mapsto\tilde p(x)$.
Nor did anything else we tried: neither $400$ random directions nor a coordinate
search within the linear class, nor --- searching over rankings directly, outside
any parametric class --- structured families such as $\logit\tilde p + c\,\delta$
or several thousand steps of pairwise-swap ascent, which bought $+0.0003$ AUROC.
The plug-in rankings by $\plo$, $\pup$, $\tilde p$ and $p_1$ all land within
$0.003$ of each other, while ranking by the identification width $\delta$ alone
is near chance --- a sanity check that the exact oracle is measuring what we think.

We therefore do \emph{not} recommend a special ranking-robust training
procedure: rank by the midpoint of the identified set. Whether
$x\mapsto\tilde p(x)$ is exactly optimal for worst-case AUROC --- as the evidence
suggests but we have not been able to prove --- is, to us, the most interesting
question this paper leaves open. \Cref{thm:sharp-auc} makes it a well-posed one:
the objective can be evaluated exactly, so any proposed optimum can be checked.
