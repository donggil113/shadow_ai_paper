\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\Cref{tab:related} (\cref{app:related-matrix}) positions the paper; the
extended discussion is in \cref{app:related-full}. In brief: contraction
\citep{lakkaraju2017selective, kleinberg2018human} uses leniency variation to
\emph{identify a point} and needs the most lenient decision maker to accept at
least the target rate, whereas we characterise everything the data cannot
rule out and use leniency to \emph{falsify} $\Gamma$; $\dcsm(\Gamma)$ is the
decision-censoring form of Rosenbaum's bounds and of Tan's marginal
sensitivity model \citep{rosenbaum2002observational, tan2006distributional},
for which sharp bounds exist for weighted averages and effects
\citep{dorn2023sharp, zhao2019sensitivity} but not for a ranking metric, and
whose closest learning antecedent \citep{kallus2018confounding} targets policy
value rather than predictive risk and needs a relaxation where our inner
supremum is closed-form; IPW and doubly robust estimation
\citep{robins1994estimation, chernozhukov2018double} assume $\Gamma=1$ and
selection models \citep{heckman1979sample} assume joint normality; the rank
identity is a population form of the Mann--Whitney representation
\citep{mann1947test} not previously used to linearise AUROC in the outcome
regression; and the credal decomposition of \cref{cor:uq} follows
\citet{abellan2005difference}.
