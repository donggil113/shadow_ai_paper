\title{\bf Decision-Censored Learning:\\
Sharp Ranking Bounds and Minimax Prediction under Selective Labels}

\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
In a large class of consequential prediction problems the label is produced only
where a human or incumbent policy decided to look. Loans are repaid only if
granted, diagnoses are recorded only if a test was ordered, recidivism is
observed only for defendants who were released. The decision maker acts on
private information $S$ that never reaches the training set, so the labelled
sub-population is not a random sample of the deployment population and the
deployment risk of a model is \emph{not identified}. We study learning in this
regime, which we call \emph{decision-censored}, under a sensitivity model
$\dcsm(\Gamma)$ that bounds by $\log\Gamma$ how far the censored units' outcome
log-odds may differ from the labelled units'.
%
Our first result is an exact rank identity showing that AUROC, though a
non-convex bilinear-fractional functional of the outcome regression, becomes
\emph{linear} once the prevalence is fixed. This yields what is, to our knowledge, the
first \emph{sharp} partial-identification interval for a ranking metric under a
sensitivity model, computable exactly in $O(n\log n)$; prior work bounds only fixed-threshold
metrics, which we recover as a special case. The recipe a practitioner would
reach for --- evaluating AUROC at the corners of the identified set --- turns out
not merely to be loose but to be \emph{invalid}, covering the truth in $10\%$ of
our benchmark cells against $100\%$ for the sharp interval.
%
Our second result turns the identified set into a learning algorithm. The worst
case over the identified set admits a closed form --- the risk at the midpoint
model plus a margin penalty weighted by the local identification width --- which
is \emph{exactly convex}, so no relaxation is needed, and which has a saddle
point and a closed-form Bayes act: an interval-shrunk logit with an abstention
band. A companion minimax-\emph{regret} rule has its own closed form, a
difference quotient of the binary entropy, and in experiments costs almost
nothing in realised accuracy while cutting certified regret several-fold.
%
Third, we show $\Gamma$ is unavoidable: no estimator is consistent for
deployment risk uniformly over censoring mechanisms, for any sample size. But
the news is not purely negative --- with leniency variation across decision
makers, $\Gamma$ becomes \emph{falsifiable} from below; our test is valid in all
30 benchmark configurations, never refutes a genuinely missing-at-random
mechanism, and recovers a third to two thirds of $\log\Gamma_0$.
%
Fourth, a generalisation bound whose complexity term is linear in $\log\Gamma$,
matching the empirical scaling.
%
Finally, a corollary for uncertainty quantification: under decision censoring
the aleatoric/epistemic decomposition is biased, the bias is \emph{undetectable}
from observed data, and the epistemic term vanishes with $n$ while identification
uncertainty does not --- by $n=70{,}000$ the reported epistemic uncertainty is
two orders of magnitude smaller than the censoring term it omits.
%
We evaluate on a synthetic benchmark with exact oracle quantities, real Lending
Club credit data with the underwriter's own risk assessment held out, a
simulated MIMIC-IV emergency-department cohort, and --- as the one case where the
censored labels were recorded anyway --- real pretrial release decisions from
COMPAS, where the sharp interval attains $100\%$ coverage exactly at the
$\Gamma$ implied by the data's own odds ratio while the missing-at-random
assumption covers $0\%$.
\end{abstract}
